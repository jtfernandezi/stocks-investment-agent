### Lever 4 — Orchestrator judgment 🔴 (the core finding)
Every scheduled summary 06-15→06-17 says the same thing in different words: *"we deliberately reject a wide range of new TREND-based opportunities across nuclear, copper, semis, data centers, SaaS, and financials, as the feedback system shows negative expected value for this pattern and our last four such trades have all lost money."*

The evidence the orchestrator is acting on:
- `pattern_performance` shows **TREND: 2 trades, 0 wins, EV −3.49%** and **NOISE: 1 trade, EV −2.83%.** These are the only "negative" patterns, and both are below any reasonable sample floor.
- The orchestrator's prompt **contradicts itself** on how to treat this:
  - **§7b (lines ~481-485)** correctly says: apply EV rules "ONLY to patterns with ≥5 closed trades … Fewer than 5 trades → EV is unreliable; IGNORE it … Never block a trade on a negative EV derived from fewer than 5 trades — early in the experiment this would freeze the book."

- The orchestrator obeyed the louder, floor-less signals (the inline "DO NOT TRADE" flag + §4d's "overrides prior"), and froze the book.
- **The cruelest irony:** the fund's *open winners* — GD (+6.3%), RTX (+7.4%), PANW (+5.5%) — are all `entry_pattern = TREND`. The pattern the orchestrator condemned as un-tradeable is the pattern carrying its entire positive P&L. The "0% win rate" verdict came from 2 losing cold-start trades, not from TREND being a bad pattern.

This is the single highest-leverage problem in the system right now. See Changes Shipped (the §7b annotation half, coded `[A]` PR #7) and Spec-only (the §4d behavioral half, `[B-spec]` Backlog #8).
